\section{Randomized approximation algorithms}
The use of randomization can help achieve simple and good approximations to hard optimization problems.
\subsection{Random choices and coin flips}
\begin{notesdefinition}
A RANDOMIZED ALGORITHM is an algorithm which may use the primitive RANDOM$(S)$, where $S$ is any finite set.
$X=\mathrm{RANDOM}(S)\in S$ is a random variable returning any element of $S$ with probability $1/|S|$ (uniform distr. on $S$).
\end{notesdefinition}
Different runs of RANDOM:
$X=\mathrm{RANDOM}(S_1)$; $Y=\mathrm{RANDOM}(S_2)$ are probabilistically independent, in the sense that:
\[\forall x\in S_1,\forall y\in S_2:
\Pr((X=x)\wedge(Y=y))=\frac1{|S_1|}\cdot\frac1{|S_2|}.\]
$x\gets\mathrm{RANDOM}(S)$: extraction from urn $S$ with replacement.
\begin{notesdefinition}
SPECIAL CASE

$X=\mathrm{RANDOM}(\{0,1\})$ ($\Pr(X=0)=\Pr(X=1)=1/2$) is called a COIN FLIP.
\end{notesdefinition}
