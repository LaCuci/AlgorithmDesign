\sourceheading{RESTRICTION: METRIC COST FUNCTION}
\begin{notesdefinition}
\textbf{DEF:} A symmetric function $c:V\times V\to\mathbb N$ is metric if it satisfies the triangle inequality:
\[\forall u,v,w:c(u,v)\leq c(u,w)+c(w,v).\]
\end{notesdefinition}
Triangle ($\triangle$) inequality states that it is always more convenient to go directly from $u$ to $v$ rather than via intermediate nodes. E.g.: nodes $u,v$ are points in $\mathbb R^d$ and $c(u,v)$ is their Euclidean distance.

The decision problem TRIANGLE-TSP is defined as TSP: the instance is $\langle G,c,K\rangle$ with the additional constraint that $c$ is metric.
\begin{notestheorem}
\textbf{THEOREM} $\mathrm{TRIANGLE\text{-}TSP}\in\classNPH$.
\end{notestheorem}
\textbf{PROOF} We reduce from TSP as follows:
\[\langle G=(V,E),c,K\rangle\overset f\longmapsto\langle G=(V,E),c',K'\rangle.\]
Let $W=\max\{c(u,v):u,v\in V\}$:
\[c'(u,v)=\begin{cases}c(u,v)+W&u\ne v\in V,\\0&u=v\in V,\end{cases}\]
\[K'=K+|V|\cdot W.\]
