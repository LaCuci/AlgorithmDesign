if x is accepted by a language in NP \textbf{if and only if}  a certificate y exists such that y is small and the verification algorithm for L returns 1 for (x,y) \textbf{if and only if} our circuit built for VL on x returns 1 for the certificate y  \textbf{if and only if} the circuit for VL on x is satisfiable (so belongs to BC SAT)  \textbf{if and only if} the output of the reduction function from L to BC SAT given when x is the input belongs to BC SAT 

Formally we have :
\[
\begin{aligned}
x\in L&\Longleftrightarrow\exists\bar y,\ |\bar y|=O(|x|^{k_1}):\ V_L(x,\bar y)=1\\
&\Longleftrightarrow C_{V_L,x}(\bar y)=1\\
&\qquad\{C_{V_L,x}\text{ simulates }V_L(x,y)\}\\
&\Longleftrightarrow\langle C_{V_L,x}\rangle\in\mathrm{BC\text{-}SAT}\\
&\Longleftrightarrow f_{L\to\mathrm{BC\text{-}SAT}}(x)\in\mathrm{BC\text{-}SAT}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus $L\polyred\mathrm{BC\text{-}SAT}$.
This proves that $\mathrm{BC\text{-}SAT}\in\classNPH$.

We have come up with a uniform reduction of a general language in NP to a language associated to boolean circuit satisfiability. 

In what follows we will prove that a number of problems are in NPC. The details of the reductions will teach us general approaches and highlight pitfalls to avoid.
