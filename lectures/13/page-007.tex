Clearly:
\begin{align*}
&\langle G=(V,E),K\rangle\in\mathrm{VC}\\
\Longleftrightarrow\quad&\exists V'\subseteq V,\ |V'|=K:\quad
\forall e=\{u,v\}\in E:(u\in V')\vee(v\in V')\\
\Longleftrightarrow\quad&\exists V'\subseteq V,\ |V'|=K:\quad
\forall e\in E\ \exists u\in V':e\in N_u\\
\Longleftrightarrow\quad&\exists V'\subseteq V,\ |V'|=K:\quad E=\bigcup_{u\in V'}N_u\\
\Longleftrightarrow\quad&\exists\mathcal C'\subseteq\mathcal F_G,\ |\mathcal C'|=K'=K:\quad
X_G=E=\bigcup_{S\in\mathcal C'}S\\[-2pt]
&\hspace{3em}(\mathcal C'=\{N_u:u\in V'\})\\
\Longleftrightarrow\quad&f(\langle G=(V,E),K\rangle)=\langle E_G,\mathcal F_G,K'\rangle\in\mathrm{SC}.
\end{align*}
\subsection{The greedy choice}
SC admits the following, intuitive greedy algorithm:
\begin{itemize}
\item GC: Choose the subset $S\in\mathcal F$ covering the largest number of elements of $X$.
\item Cleanup: Remove covered elements.
\end{itemize}
(REMARK: For VC this corresponds to select the node of largest degree.)
