Due to the greedy choice A-S-C selects
$S_t=\operatorname{argmax}\{|T\cap U_t|:T\in\mathcal F\}$.
Thus $|U_t\cap S_t|\geq|U_t\cap S_{i_{\bar j}}|\geq|U_t|/K$.
\begin{notesresult}
\textbf{Morale:} Thanks to the greedy choice I always cover a fraction
$\geq1/K=1/|\mathcal C^*|$ of the yet uncovered elements!
\end{notesresult}
This gives the important relation between the returned solution and the optimal solution.

We can write the following recurrence:
\[\begin{cases}|U_1|=n,\\
|U_{t+1}|\leq|U_t|-|U_t|/K=|U_t|(1-1/K).
\end{cases}\]
By unfolding:
\begin{align*}
|U_{t+1}|&\leq|U_t|(1-1/K)\leq|U_{t-1}|(1-1/K)^2\\
&\leq|U_{t-i}|(1-1/K)^{i+1}\\
&\overset{i=t-1}{\leq}|U_1|(1-1/K)^t=n(1-1/K)^t.
\end{align*}
$|U_{t+1}|$: uncovered elements after iteration $t$.
